The Gopher Care Guide

A little dirt goes a long way. Here's how to keep your new burrower happy, healthy, and gainfully tunneling.

🏙️

Burrow Setup

Gophers are homebodies — undergroundbodies, really. Provide at least 18 inches of loosely packed, diggable substrate (a mix of soil and sand works wonders). Include a multi-chamber tunnel system with a dedicated "nesting nook," a "pantry," and, yes, a "bathroom" — gophers are surprisingly tidy about separating the two.

A secure, escape-proof enclosure is non-negotiable. These are professional-grade excavators. Chicken wire buried a foot down along the perimeter keeps your gopher in and your prized tulip bed... well, that's between you and them.

🍃

Diet

Gophers are herbivorous grazers with a taste for the finer root vegetables. A balanced diet includes roots, tubers, clover, bulbs, and the occasional carrot as a treat. Fresh greens 2–3 times per week keep the digestive tunnel (their word, not ours) running smoothly.

Avoid processed snacks and anything from the nightshade family in large quantities. A shallow dish of water should be available at all times, even though most hydration comes from their food.

✋

Exercise Needs

Digging isn't just a hobby, it's a biological necessity. Provide a rotating "dig zone" of fresh loose soil weekly to satisfy their instinct to tunnel — and to save your houseplants from becoming collateral landscaping projects.

Supervised above-ground time in a secure playpen helps with socialization. Most gophers enjoy short bursts of exploring, followed by an immediate and enthusiastic return underground. Tunnel vision, remember?

⚠️

Safety Notes

Gophers have strong front teeth built for chewing through roots — and, occasionally, unattended furniture legs. Gopher-proof your space accordingly.

Never house two unfamiliar adult gophers together without a proper introduction period; territorial disputes are settled with dramatic dirt-kicking, not violence, but it's still best avoided. Keep enclosures away from direct sun and always supervise interactions with other pets — cats find "surprise burrow exits" far more thrilling than gophers do.
